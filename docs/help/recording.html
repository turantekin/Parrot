Recording a call

Press the big red button on the dashboard (or use the menu bar icon) and Parrot starts recording two tracks at once: the system audio, which is everyone else on the call, and your microphone, which is you. Because the tracks are separate, the transcript already knows who said what.

If the Copilot is on, a card under the record button holds everything it will know from the first second: pick a call profile, type a brief for this call, and check the documents in play. The brief is a line or two in your own words, like "renewal call with Northwind, legal wants to know where the data is stored". The documents line shows which files the Copilot can quote for the chosen profile, and the Knowledge link next to it takes you straight to the place to add more. Profiles are explained in Call profiles, documents in Knowledge base.

Parrot can notice calls for you

When Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, FaceTime or any other app starts using your microphone, Parrot notices the call and a notification asks "Zoom call started. Record it with Parrot?" The same question shows as a banner at the top of Parrot's window and in the menu bar icon, so you'll see it even with notifications turned off. When the call app lets go of the mic, Parrot asks whether to stop too. Parrot only sees that the mic is in use (the same thing the orange dot in the menu bar shows), never the audio of another app.

Pick what happens in Settings → Recording → Call Detection: Ask me (the default), Record automatically, or Off. In automatic mode Parrot starts on its own and stops when the call ends. Recording laws differ by place, and many require telling everyone on the call, so automatic mode puts that on you. If an app keeps getting mistaken for a call (a dictation tool, say), choose Never for This App on the notification. Settings lists those apps with an "Ask Again" button.

Detection only works while Parrot is running, so turn on Open Parrot at login in Settings → General.

Your calendar names the meeting

Connect your calendar in Settings → Recording → Calendar (or in the welcome tour) and a call that lines up with a calendar event takes its name and guest list. Guests show up as one-click name suggestions when you name the voices after the call. For a call Parrot noticed itself, an event title like "Interview: Jane" or "Acme demo" also picks the matching call profile, but only when exactly one profile fits. Parrot uses the calendars already on your Mac (iCloud, Google, Outlook), reads them and never changes them.

Two extra switches appear once it's connected. Brief the copilot from the invite lets the live Copilot read the event's title, guests and notes. It's off by default because that's someone else's writing, and a cloud Copilot would receive it. Remind me before meetings sends a notification a minute before a meeting with guests or a video link.

While you're recording

The bar at the top shows your input and output devices and a live level meter for each track. If you switch AirPods mid-call or your Bluetooth headphones die, Parrot rebuilds the microphone connection on its own and keeps going.

One warning is worth knowing about: if the text "mic muted by another app" appears in orange, it means your call app grabbed exclusive control of the microphone and macOS is feeding Parrot silence. Parrot keeps trying to reclaim it every few seconds and usually gets it back. The other side of the call keeps recording the whole time, so worst case you lose some of your own words, not the meeting. There's more on this in Troubleshooting.

Telling people they're recorded

The Consent button at the top of the call screen copies a short notice ("Heads-up: I'm recording this call with Parrot…") to paste into the call chat, or notes that everyone agreed out loud. Either way the meeting keeps a record of it, shown under its title and in exports. Edit the notice in Settings → Privacy. With the reminder on, the button stays orange until you've used it.

Marking moments

Something important just happened? Click Mark at the top of the call screen, choose Recording → Mark Moment, use the menu bar icon, or press ⌃⌥M from any app, even while Zoom or your browser is in front. The button offers a box to say what it was ("pricing question"); typing is optional. Marked moments show up in the report under Moments you marked, and the report is written knowing they mattered to you. After the call you can also right-click any transcript line and choose Bookmark This Line.

The ⌃⌥M shortcut only exists while a recording is running, and it's the only key Parrot can see. Turn it off in Settings → Recording → Bookmarks.

Stopping

Hit Stop and Parrot finishes writing the last words, saves both audio files, and starts working on the report in the background. You can open the meeting straight away and watch it fill in.

Forgot to stop?

If nobody has spoken for 15 minutes, Parrot sends a "Still recording?" notification, and again every 15 minutes after that until someone talks or you stop. macOS asks once whether Parrot may send notifications; if you say no, the Dock icon bounces instead. A silent room isn't written down as words, so a forgotten recording only costs disk space. To cut off anything after the call, right-click a line and choose Delete Everything After This Line.

Quitting mid-call is fine

If you quit Parrot while recording, it finishes the recording first and then quits. And if your Mac crashes or the app is force-quit mid-call, the next launch finds the interrupted meeting and rescues it: the transcript that was already written is kept, speakers are re-checked, and the report runs as usual. You lose nothing that was already said.
