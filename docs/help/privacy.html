What leaves your Mac

Short version: with a fresh install, nothing of yours. Every choice that changes that is one you make yourself, and here is the complete list.

Always on your Mac

  • Audio recordings. They are written to your disk and never uploaded anywhere by anything, ever. No exceptions, no telemetry, no accounts.
  • Transcription, by default: the Whisper model runs on your machine and works with the network cable unplugged.
  • Meetings, transcripts, insights, reports: a local database in the app's own container.
  • Your API keys, if you add any: stored in the macOS Keychain, not in files.
  • Your documents: read, split up and indexed on your Mac. The files themselves are never uploaded.
  • Your calendar, if you connect it: read on your Mac through macOS's own calendar store, never changed. Meeting names and guest lists are saved with your meetings, locally.
  • Call detection: Parrot asks macOS which apps are using the microphone (the orange-dot information). It never hears their audio.

Downloads

Parrot downloads models once, the first time it needs them: Whisper, voice detection and speaker detection models from Hugging Face, and for Arabic, Indic and sometimes Cyrillic documents, a language model from Apple. These are plain downloads. Nothing about you, your calls or your documents goes with them, and after that everything runs offline.

Sent out only if you set it up

  • Copilot on Claude or a custom server: transcript text from the current call (plus matched knowledge base snippets and your profile's instructions) goes to that provider during calls and for reports. Audio never goes.
  • Instant document answers (TypeSafe AI): only with a TypeSafe key and only when the Copilot runs on Claude. When the other side asks a question, that question, the last couple of lines of the call, and the matching snippets of your documents go to TypeSafe AI, hosted in the United States, so the matching excerpt can show within a second. Audio never goes. TypeSafe states it does not train on what you send and keeps it only as long as needed to run the service.
  • Calendar invites for the Copilot: only if you turn on "Brief the copilot from the invite" and the Copilot runs on Claude or a custom server. The matched event's title, guest names and notes (minus dial-in links and numbers) go with the Copilot's requests during that call. Off by default.
  • Cloud transcription (Groq or Deepgram): the call audio is sent to them for speech-to-text as it happens. This is the one opt-in that sends sound. On-device stays the default.
  • Update check: once a day Parrot reads its update feed on GitHub Pages. The request carries the app's version and nothing about you or your calls. Updates are signed, download in the background and install when you quit, never during a recording. Turn off Keep Parrot up to date in Settings → General to install by hand.

And Ollama?

Ollama runs on your own machine, so a Copilot on Ollama keeps everything local: Parrot only ever talks to it on localhost. Local model, local transcription, zero outbound bytes. That combination is the whole reason Parrot exists.
